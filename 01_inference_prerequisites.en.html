1

Shared inference vs dedicated deployment: the break-even point

Model: Cost = min(Tokens × Rate, GPU_Hours × Wage)In ledger terms: per-parcel courier fees vs a leased cargo truck

In ledger terms (cost trade-off)

When a startup's order volume is small, outsourcing delivery at 3,000 won per parcel wins. But once daily shipments reach tens of thousands, a dedicated dispatch — leased trucks and hired drivers — becomes far cheaper.

In neural-network infrastructure

Early on, a per-token public API (Shared Endpoint) is safe with zero engineering overhead. But as traffic grows, dedicated serving on rented GPU instances cuts the per-token price to a fifth or less.

At the current traffic level, dedicated deployment saves about $420 per month versus the shared API. You have crossed the break-even point!

Ledger math for beginners

  1. Strengths of shared inference: no cold start, pay only for what you use, and early engineering resources are saved.
  2. Limits of shared inference: usage and cost explode linearly 1:1, and noisy neighbors make latency guarantees impossible.
  3. Top 3 reasons to go dedicated: scale, specialized model operation, and orchestration of compound pipelines.
In the field Building your own vLLM cluster at the bare-prototype stage wastes millions of won a month on idle GPU costs. Until you reach product-market fit, secure agility with a commercial API. Where the analogy breaks: dedicated GPUs add server downtime and deployment maintenance labor.

2

Domain specialization: fine-tuning and knowledge distillation

Strategy: 671B generalist ➔ 3B tailored lightweightIn ledger terms: skip the universal genius, hire a dedicated interpreter

In ledger terms

Paying a top-priced star scholar who knows every language, the humanities, and coding just to write in-house SQL queries is a serious payroll leak. Training a junior with basic language skills on the in-house DB schema alone is overwhelmingly faster and cheaper.

In neural-network infrastructure

Trillion-parameter frontier models consume too much inference cost and memory bandwidth. In closed domains like Text-to-SQL, fine-tune a small 3B model or distill from a large teacher model to reach comparable accuracy.

Test data: 500 real business queries
Test complete: The 3B fine-tuned model keeps accuracy at 97.4% of the 671B frontier model while cutting inference cost by 92%!

Fine-tuning vs knowledge distillation: the key difference

  1. Fine-tuning: keeps the architecture and weight count while updating weights toward domain answers on a domain dataset.
  2. Distillation: transfers the large teacher model's internal probability distributions (logits) themselves to teach a small student model its behavior.
  3. Goodhart's Law: do not drown in generic benchmark scores like MMLU — build your own Evals based on your service's real tasks.
In generative AI Right after DeepSeek-R1 (671B) launched, open models distilled from Llama-3 8B exploded in popularity in the open-source ecosystem — because they are small enough to fly on a single GPU. Where the analogy breaks: a distilled model may absorb the teacher model's biases and wrong-answer patterns as-is.
Money-Robo

But look at the majesty of the hundreds of billions of tokens a 671B model pours out — doesn't the investor's blood boil? Size is power!

3

Latency vs throughput — and tail latency (P99)

Metrics: TTFT, TPS, P50, P99In ledger terms: express VIP counter vs overnight bulk settlement batch

In ledger terms

At a bank, a clerk instantly serving one VIP customer is 'latency optimization'; bundling millions of transfers in the computer room at night and clearing them in one batch is 'throughput optimization'. The design philosophies are completely different.

In neural-network infrastructure

For a chatbot, time to first token (TTFT) and tokens per second (TPS) are life itself. Offline jobs like podcast transcription or document embedding, by contrast, sacrifice per-item latency and maximize batch size to maximize throughput per GPU.

Concurrent users: 16
Serving mode:
Latency metric analysis: the median (P50) is a comfy 180ms, but queuing pushes the P99 tail latency up to 1,240ms. One user in a hundred suspects the service is down!

Why you must watch the tail (right-skewed distribution)

  1. The average trap: LLM response times have a long right tail, not a normal distribution. Looking only at the average hides the pain of outliers.
  2. P90 / P99 management: bottleneck latency hitting 1 in 10 or 1 in 100 requests destroys user trust.
  3. TTFT vs TPS split: TTFT is compute-bound (prefill — reading the prompt at once); TPS is memory-bandwidth-bound (decode — continuously reading weights from memory).
